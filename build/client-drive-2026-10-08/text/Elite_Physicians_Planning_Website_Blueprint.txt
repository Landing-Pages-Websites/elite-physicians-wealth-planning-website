1. Executive Direction
Recommended position: Elite Physicians Planning should be presented as a specialized planning experience for physicians and medical professionals who want tax, retirement, investment, risk, and legacy decisions coordinated around one financial strategy.

Brand promise
Help physicians convert strong earnings into durable wealth, greater financial clarity, and a coordinated path toward retirement and legacy goals—without asking them to become their own financial planner.
Primary audience
	•	Established physicians and surgeons balancing high income, limited time, and complex financial decisions.
	•	Physician practice owners and partners seeking integrated personal and business planning.
	•	Physicians within 10 years of retirement who need income, tax, risk, and estate decisions coordinated.
	•	Residents, fellows, and early-career physicians building the right foundation as income rises.
	•	Select medical professionals whose planning needs align with the initiative’s scope.
Positioning statement
Elite Physicians Planning is a specialized financial planning initiative of M A Epps & Associates. It brings tax strategy, retirement planning, wealth management, risk protection, and estate coordination into one disciplined planning process designed for the financial realities of medical careers.
Messaging guardrails
	•	Lead with coordination, clarity, and physician-specific planning—not products.
	•	Describe tax work as forward-looking tax planning coordinated with the client’s tax professional; do not imply tax-return preparation unless that service is actually provided.
	•	Avoid guarantees, superiority claims, unverified savings claims, or language suggesting every physician needs the same strategy.
	•	Use a calm, private, evidence-conscious tone. The site should feel like a trusted professional consultation, not a sales funnel.
	•	Treat insurance, investment, tax, and estate topics as parts of a plan, with role disclosures and professional coordination clearly stated.
Recommended headline system
Primary headline: Build Wealth With the Same Precision You Bring to Medicine.
Supporting line: Integrated tax, retirement, and wealth planning for physicians and medical professionals.
Primary call to action: Request a Private Consultation
Secondary call to action: Complete the Physician Financial Checkup
Success measures
	•	Qualified private-consultation requests by physician career stage and planning need.
	•	Completion rate for the Physician Financial Checkup and subsequent consultation conversion.
	•	Engagement with tax, retirement, practice-owner, and near-retirement content.
	•	Referral traffic from CPAs, attorneys, TPAs, medical associations, and existing clients.
	•	Compliance approval cycle, publication cadence, accessibility, speed, and mobile conversion performance.

	•	Career transitions, partnership opportunities, practice ownership, and retirement can arrive before the financial plan is ready.
	•	A physician’s family needs clarity about the plan, not a collection of disconnected accounts and policies.

	•	Practice Owners & Partners — Align business cash flow, retirement benefits, succession, and personal wealth.

Recommended headline: One strategy. Six connected planning disciplines.
	•	Tax Strategy — Identify planning opportunities and coordinate decisions with the client’s tax professional.
	•	Retirement Planning — Define the target, evaluate resources, and build an income and transition strategy.
	•	Wealth Management — Align investments with goals, time horizon, risk, taxes, and liquidity needs.

	•	Implement — Coordinate accounts, planning actions, and outside professionals with the client’s approval.
	•	Optimize — Review the strategy as compensation, family, practice, markets, tax law, and retirement goals change.

6. Our Process Page
Hero: A disciplined process for decisions that should not be made in isolation.
The page should reduce uncertainty about engagement and show that coordination is a core part of the value.
01  Identify
Begin with a private discovery conversation and Physician Financial Checkup. Clarify what is working, what has changed, and which decisions carry the greatest consequence.
Typical outputs: Priority list; document request; planning scope; roles and responsibilities
02  Design
Analyze the household, career, practice, tax, retirement, investment, protection, and legacy picture as one system. Present choices, assumptions, tradeoffs, and an action sequence.
Typical outputs: Integrated strategy; scenario comparisons; implementation roadmap
03  Implement
Move approved actions forward and coordinate with the client’s CPA, attorney, TPA, insurance professionals, custodian, and other specialists as appropriate.
Typical outputs: Action tracker; professional coordination; account/policy/plan implementation
04  Optimize
Review the plan as income, tax law, markets, family, practice ownership, and retirement timing evolve.
Typical outputs: Periodic review; progress updates; annual planning calendar; revised priorities
Engagement clarity to add before launch
	•	Who is eligible and whether the relationship begins with planning, investment management, insurance, or a defined package.
	•	Fee and compensation explanation in plain language, reviewed for accuracy and consistency with disclosures.
	•	Expected meeting cadence, document requirements, and typical response times.
	•	How professional coordination works and what requires separate engagement with a CPA or attorney.
	•	How a prospect can stop, ask questions, or decide not to proceed without pressure.

12. Compliance & Content Governance
Launch principle: Treat website copy, lead magnets, videos, calculators, testimonials, endorsements, third-party ratings, and social promotion as regulated marketing content requiring the firm’s normal supervisory and recordkeeping process.

Pre-publication review checklist
	•	Verify firm name, registrations, service descriptions, licensing, professional titles, credentials, fee language, and service area.
	•	Substantiate every factual claim, statistic, comparison, award, rating, outcome, and statement about experience.
	•	Review testimonials, endorsements, and third-party ratings for eligibility, required disclosures, oversight, and written agreements where applicable.
	•	Do not publish performance results, extracted results, hypothetical performance, tax-savings examples, or client outcomes without a dedicated review of methodology, presentation, assumptions, and disclosures.
	•	Confirm Form CRS and regulatory-document delivery or linking requirements that apply to the firm’s registration and visitor journey.
	•	Place tax, legal, investment, and insurance disclosures near the relevant content and maintain complete disclosures in the footer.
	•	Archive approved copy, evidence, reviewer, date, published version, and later revisions.
	•	Review privacy, cookie, accessibility, cybersecurity, vendor, scheduling, form, CRM, and data-retention practices.
Recommended footer disclosure framework
Final wording must come from the firm’s compliance team. At minimum, the footer should distinguish educational information from individualized advice; explain that tax and legal matters require qualified professionals; identify investment and insurance roles; state that investing involves risk; avoid any implied guarantee; and provide current regulatory and privacy links.
Testimonials and proof
The SEC’s investment adviser marketing framework permits testimonials and endorsements under specified conditions, including disclosures and oversight. Because implementation depends on the firm’s registration, compensation, promoter relationships, and exact presentation, add these elements only after formal approval. A clean first launch can rely on verified credentials, process transparency, professional coordination, and educational content instead.
